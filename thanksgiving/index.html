One choice, two questions, and beds

Dinner is a choice between two places. The spa and a second brunch are yes-or-nos. Then there's who sleeps where. Prices are the good version of each rather than the cheapest. Click any photo for full size.

The one thing that actually matters

A comfortable, tasty, stress-free Thanksgiving dinner for eight, where none of the eight of us has to cook, serve, or clean up.

Planning around the pups

Five dogs between us. Mom's one and Sis's two are housetrained and could travel at a pinch. It's our two puppies that can't, so the whole crew stays put. Easy enough to plan around:

  • Dinner somewhere else. Five dogs and a long sit-down meal wouldn't be much fun for anyone, dogs included.
  • No rush to get back. They're happy in the pen for up to eight hours, so the day can run as long as it likes.
  • Distance only counts for where we sleep. One drive out and home again is easy. It's days of back-and-forth that would get old.

Pick oneThanksgiving dinner

Both cost about the same, roughly $2,250 all told, and at both, nobody at the table cooks, serves, or clears. The real difference: the firehouse is ours for the whole day; Vizcaya is a three-hour dinner.

The Firehouse

~$2,260house 8 hrs $1,224 + chef ~$1,030

A real 1917 fire station in Curtis Park, firepole and all, ours from late morning to late evening, with a chef arriving partway through to shop, cook, serve and clear. A whole day of somewhere that's ours. Chef Maggie lists Thanksgiving as a speciality (5.0, 41 bookings); Chef Karen does French fine dining (4.8, 134).

Before we book Ask whether an outside chef may cook there, and whether Thanksgiving Day is bookable. Neither is stated on the listing.

Vizcaya

~$2,230Coach House $750 + plated $71/head + drinks + 22%

An 1889 Midtown mansion with a Victorian tower, where their own kitchen team cooks and their staff serve. A proper plated dinner, nothing for us to arrange beyond the menu. Three hours in the Coach House. And if we take the room downstairs anyway, dinner is a walk upstairs from bed.

Yes or noTwo extras, if we fancy them

Neither of these is a fork in the road. They're each a straight yes or no, and they don't affect the dinner either way. Say no to both and the plan still works.

Do we spa?

A boutique day spa inside the 1936 Fong Mansion downtown, with an infrared sauna, steam room, relaxation lounge, robes and sandals. Their concierge builds group bookings, so it's one arrangement rather than eight appointments. Friday or Saturday afternoon.

$920–2,790 for eight · $115–349 a head depending on treatment

The full version is the Indulgence: a facial and hour massage each, $2,792 for all of us. A 90-minute deep tissue each is $1,912. Anyone staying at the inn gets 10% off.

Menu & booking → The spa → 916-658-1818

Holiday-week hours need confirming, group bookings want 4+ days' notice, and cancellations inside 24 hours are charged in full.

Do we brunch on another day?

Grand Island Mansion, a restored 1920s estate on the river at Walnut Grove, 35 minutes south, and the most striking building of everywhere we looked. Champagne buffet: prime rib, seafood, eggs benedict, dessert table, champagne kept topped up. Tables are held to 90 minutes, which is why it suits a second day rather than Thanksgiving itself.

~$811 for eight on a Sunday · ~$1,014 on Thanksgiving Day · includes the automatic 18% for parties of 8+

The Sunday option means staying through Sunday the 29th, a fourth night, so this question and the dates question are the same question. Seatings 10:30–1:30, booked on OpenTable, $50 a head to cancel. Dress code unpublished; ask.

Then bedsWho sleeps where

The kids are covered with family either way. The question is whether it's just the two of us taking a room, or whether Mom fancies a hotel stay as part of the treat.

Just a room for the two of us

$330–690 / 3 nights

Two kids at Sis's, one at Mom's, and we take a room. The nice version is Vizcaya: a Carriage House room with a fireplace and soaking tub, or the Penthouse for the whole top floor. Rooms book like any B&B ($109–230/night, breakfast included), and if dinner is there too we're sleeping upstairs from our own Thanksgiving.

The charming-but-cheaper version is the IreneHouse Victorian Inn: an actual Victorian, the best-reviewed place on this whole page (9.2 from 294), at $135 a night.
