Two decisions, not one

Where we eat and where we sleep turn out to follow completely different rules, so this page treats them separately. Click any photo to see it full size; every place links through to its own page so you can look properly and make up your own mind.

The one thing that actually matters

A comfortable, tasty, stress-free Thanksgiving dinner for eight, where none of the eight of us has to cook, serve, or clean up.

The dogs set the rules — and they split the problem in two

Five dogs, two of them puppies, permanently at one house, and whoever looks after them isn't sleeping away from them. That isn't a preference to design around; it's the fixed point everything else fits. Working it through:

  • No pet-friendly anything required. The dogs don't travel, so every rental's pet policy is beside the point.
  • Dinner can't be where the dogs are. Five of them circling a long sit-down meal is the opposite of relaxing — and comfortable was the first word in what we said we wanted.
  • Time away isn't a problem. They pen happily for up to eight hours. No rota, no slipping out between courses, no cutting the day short.
  • Distance is fine once, fatal repeated. One drive out to dinner and back is nothing, even at half an hour. It's three or four days of shuttling that breaks.

Which means dinner can be anywhere good, and only the beds need to be close. Those are genuinely separate questions, so they get separate sections below.

Decision oneWhere we eat

Needs to be dog-free and seat eight properly. Distance doesn't matter here — it's one trip out and one back — so this is the decision to make on quality alone, not on convenience.

A dog-free house, with a chef brought in

$712–952, no venue cost

Much the cheapest route to a very good dinner, because there's no room to pay for. The chef's fee covers the lot: “Chef shops for ingredients, cooks in your kitchen, you enjoy the meal, and chef cleans up.” Works equally well in a dog-free house or in whichever place we end up renting to sleep in.

The catch is the room, not the food A dog-free house that also seats eight in comfort is the thing we may not have — and the dog-free one was already described as tight on both kitchen and table. Worth measuring before ruling it in or out. A hired table for eight runs $150–300 delivered, so “not enough space” sometimes just means “not enough table”. Decision twoWhere we sleep

This got much easier once dinner moved out

The old search wanted somewhere that slept everyone and seated eight at a table. That combination barely exists in Sacramento — it's why we were left with almost nothing to choose from. Once dinner happens elsewhere, a place to stay just needs beds. Drop the dining requirement and the guest count to five, and the field goes from a handful to eighteen inside three miles, at lower prices, and including several period houses that never showed up before.

This is the one part where distance genuinely matters, because it's the bit that repeats. Sorted nearest first.
